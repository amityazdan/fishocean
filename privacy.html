Privacy Policy

Fish Ocean · updated November 7, 2026

Fish Ocean does not collect personal information, does not use analytics or advertising, and does not track you.

What stays on your Mac

Your fish (its name, size, age, journal and settings) is saved only on your Mac. From version 1.3 the app also keeps, on your Mac only, the dates of the last 14 days the ocean was on your screen, so it can choose a good moment to show a tip or ask for an App Store rating. None of this is sent anywhere.

Swimming with friends

If you're signed in to iCloud, a small amount of data about your fish is stored in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit public database), so friends can find it if you share your code: a random fish code, the fish's name and color, and when it was last online. When you add or invite a friend, the invitation between your two fish codes is stored there too. This data is not linked to your Apple ID name or email, and we cannot see who you are. From version 1.5 on the Mac and 1.1 on Windows, the record also says which kind of fish it is (for example a clownfish) and your country, taken from your computer's region setting (not from your location). As the developer we can see these records (a fish's code, name, color, kind, country, when it was first and last online, and whether it is a Mac or Windows fish), and we look at them to understand how Fish Ocean is used, to help when you ask for support, and to stop abuse. They still say nothing about who you are.

If you ask us for help changing your fish, we can leave a signed request for your fish code in the same database. Your app checks that the request really comes from us and then asks you first; nothing changes unless you click Change.

From version 1.3, when someone adds your fish's code you get a friend request, and their fish can't visit until you accept. Your friend requests and the fish you remove, decline or block are kept only on your Mac. Fish names that look offensive or contain contact details are shown as “Fish”.

While fish swim together over the internet, each app sends its fish's position and animation through our relay server (hosted on Cloudflare), which forwards it to the other fish in the same private room. The relay keeps nothing: when a room is empty it is forgotten.

The Open Ocean (optional)

From version 1.5 on the Mac and 1.1 on Windows you can turn on the Open Ocean, where fish of people you don't know pass by your screen. It is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off at any time.

While it is on, the app connects to our Open Ocean server (hosted on Cloudflare) and sends your fish's code, its name, color and kind, whether it has Full Ocean, and the width of your ocean. It also sends a random secret kept on your device; the server stores only a one-way hash of it, so no one else can join as your fish. The server uses this to choose which fish visit each other. During a visit the other person's app sees only your fish's name, color and kind, never your fish code, and the fish swim through the same relay as friends do, in a private room that is forgotten when the visit ends.

The server keeps which fish codes have met (for 90 days, so fish can meet again), each visit (7 days), and how many oceans your fish visited today (2 days). It keeps the fish codes you block, so you never meet again. If you report a fish, the report (your fish code, the other fish's code and name, and the visit) is kept for 90 days; we get a notification on our phone without any fish details, look at the report, and can stop a fish code from joining the Open Ocean. The list of fish you met is kept only on your device.

Updates

From version 1.4.2, the Mac app asks Apple's App Store once a day which version of Fish Ocean is current in your country, so it can tell you when an update is out. The request goes straight to Apple and carries no information about you or your fish.

On Windows

Fish Ocean for Windows works the same way. Your fish and settings are saved only on your PC. Windows PCs can't use iCloud directly, so the Windows app reaches the same Apple CloudKit public database through our own small server on Cloudflare, which passes each request on and stores nothing. To protect your fish code, the app keeps a random secret on your PC; CloudKit stores only a one-way hash of it, so only your app can change your fish's record. The server's logs contain only error types, never names, codes or messages. Live swimming uses the same relay as on the Mac.

Purchases

The optional in-app purchase is handled entirely by Apple on the Mac and by Microsoft on Windows. We never see payment details.

Children

Fish Ocean does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children.

Contact

Questions? Email amyzdevelopment@gmail.com. "Send Feedback…" and "Report…" in the app only open a new email in your own mail app (a report includes the reported fish's name and code and the app version); nothing is sent unless you send it.
